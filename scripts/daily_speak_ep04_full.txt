# Travel, Adventure & Exploring the World
## Daily Speak English | English Listening & Speaking Practice

**Solu:** Hello hello, wonderful listeners! Welcome back to another exciting episode of Daily Speak English, the podcast where you learn to speak English naturally, fluently, and with real confidence. I'm Solu!

**Tonu:** And I'm Tonu! We are so glad you are joining us today. Solu, take a look at me today—what do you notice?

**Solu:** Wait a minute! You have a backpack on, you're wearing comfortable walking shoes, and you've got a pair of sunglasses around your neck! Are you planning a getaway?

**Tonu:** You guessed it! I am in full adventure mode. Today, we are taking our listeners on a journey around the globe as we talk all about travel, adventure, exploring new cultures, and discovering the world!

**Solu:** Oh, what a fantastic topic! Travel is one of the most enriching experiences a human being can have. Mark Twain once wrote that travel is fatal to prejudice, bigotry, and narrow-mindedness.

**Tonu:** What a powerful quote. When you step outside your familiar environment and experience how other people live, eat, celebrate, and see the world, your mind expands in ways a textbook could never teach you.

**Solu:** So true, Tonu. So let's start with a personal question: Do you remember the very first time you traveled far away from home? What was that feeling like?

**Tonu:** Oh, I remember it like it was yesterday! I was so young, sitting on a plane for the first time, looking out of the window as the houses and cars shrank into tiny dots. I felt a mixture of intense excitement and nervous butterflies in my stomach!

**Solu:** That combination of thrill and slight fear is exactly what makes traveling so memorable! You're leaving behind your predictable routine and stepping into the unknown.

**Tonu:** Exactly! And what about you, Solu? What was your most unforgettable travel memory?

**Solu:** For me, it was taking a solo train trip through mountainous countryside. I didn't have a strict itinerary. I just got off at a small rural village, walked through cobblestone streets, and ended up having freshly baked bread with a local family who didn't even speak my native language!

**Tonu:** That sounds like a scene straight out of a movie! And that highlights such a beautiful truth about travel: the best moments are almost never the tourist attractions you see on postcards.

**Solu:** You are so right. The Eiffel Tower and the Colosseum are stunning, but the memories that stay etched in your heart are the spontaneous interactions with friendly locals, getting lost in narrow alleys, and tasting street food you've never heard of before.

**Tonu:** Speaking of getting lost, let's talk about the two main types of people who go abroad: tourists versus travelers. Solu, what is the difference in your mind?

**Solu:** That is a classic debate! In my view, a tourist visits a place to see it, while a traveler visits a place to experience it.

**Tonu:** Yes! A tourist often stays inside the comfort zone: booking big commercial resort hotels, eating only familiar food from international fast-food chains, taking selfies in front of famous landmarks, and rushing back to the tour bus.

**Solu:** While a traveler is curious about the local culture! They learn a few words in the local language, ride public buses, shop at bustling neighborhood markets, and genuinely respect local customs and traditions.

**Tonu:** I always encourage our listeners: whenever you visit a new country or city, make an effort to be a traveler, not just a tourist!

**Solu:** Now, let's talk about travel planning and travel styles. Some people love luxury travel—five-star resorts, private taxis, and fine dining. Others prefer backpacking on a shoestring budget. Which style do you lean toward, Tonu?

**Tonu:** I think I'm a balanced hybrid! I love budget travel because staying in hostels or guesthouses allows you to meet fascinating travelers from all corners of the world. You share travel stories over morning coffee and exchange tips on hidden gems.

**Solu:** Hostels and local guesthouses have such an energetic social vibe! You make lifelong friends from different continents in just three days.

**Tonu:** Exactly! But every once in a while, after several days of walking twenty thousand steps, it is nice to treat yourself to a comfortable room with a hot shower and quiet sleep.

**Solu:** Balance is key! Now, let's address something that stresses almost every traveler out: packing! Are you a light packer or do you pack your entire bedroom into three giant suitcases?

**Tonu:** I used to be a chronic overpacker, Solu! I would pack five pairs of shoes for a three-day weekend, thinking, 'What if I need hiking boots? What if I get invited to a royal gala? What if it suddenly snows in July?'

**Solu:** That is hilarious! And then you end up wearing the same comfortable t-shirt and jeans the entire trip while dragging fifty pounds of heavy luggage up steep subway stairs!

**Tonu:** It was awful! After breaking a suitcase wheel on a cobblestone street in Europe, I vowed never to overpack again. Now, I travel strictly with a carry-on backpack.

**Solu:** Traveling light is pure freedom! You don't have to wait at the baggage carousel, you don't worry about lost luggage, and you can easily walk anywhere without breaking your back.

**Tonu:** A golden packing rule that experienced travelers swear by: lay out all the clothes and money you think you need for your trip. Then, take half the clothes and twice the money!

**Solu:** That is brilliant advice! You almost never need half the clothes you pack, and there are always laundry services or laundromats available wherever you go.

**Tonu:** Now let's talk about the unexpected side of travel: when things go wrong! Because no matter how meticulously you plan, travel always has surprises.

**Solu:** Oh, definitely! Flights get delayed or canceled, trains break down, passports get misplaced, you get food poisoning, or you get completely lost in a neighborhood where no one speaks your language.

**Tonu:** Have you ever had a major travel mishap, Solu?

**Solu:** Oh, absolutely! I once missed a connecting flight in a foreign airport because our first plane was delayed by bad weather. It was midnight, customer service counters were closed, and my phone battery was at three percent!

**Tonu:** Oh no! That sounds like a nightmare scenario. What did you do?

**Solu:** At first, I felt a wave of panic. But then I took a deep breath and reminded myself: panic solves nothing. I found an airport security officer, charged my phone at an emergency station, found a nearby transit hotel, and got rebooked on the morning flight.

**Tonu:** And looking back now, it's just a funny travel story you share with friends!

**Solu:** Exactly! Travel mishaps teach us resilience, adaptability, and patience. When things don't go according to plan, you learn to roll with the punches.

**Tonu:** Let's also talk about airport English, because many English learners tell us that navigating international airports makes their heart pound with anxiety!

**Solu:** Oh, absolutely! The airport environment can feel very intimidating with armed security, strict border control officers, and hundreds of confusing departure signs.

**Tonu:** So let's walk our listeners through a typical airport conversation! Imagine you are approaching the check-in desk at the airline counter. What is the first thing the agent asks?

**Solu:** The agent usually smiles and says, 'Good morning! Where are you flying to today? May I please see your passport and ticket confirmation?'

**Tonu:** And you hand over your passport and reply, 'Good morning! I am flying to London Heathrow. Here is my passport and booking reference.'

**Tonu:** Then the agent asks the classic question about your luggage: 'Are you checking any bags today, or do you only have carry-on luggage?'

**Solu:** And if you have a suitcase, you put it on the scale and say, 'I have one checked bag and one carry-on backpack.'

**Tonu:** Then comes the big seating preference question: 'Would you prefer a window seat or an aisle seat?' Solu, what is your personal preference on long flights?

**Solu:** I am one hundred percent team aisle seat! I like having the freedom to stand up, stretch my legs, and use the restroom without having to tap my neighbor on the shoulder every two hours!

**Tonu:** See, I am the exact opposite! I am team window seat all the way. I love leaning my head against the airplane wall to sleep, and watching the clouds and city lights from thirty thousand feet is breathtaking.

**Solu:** That is the eternal airplane debate: window seat for the views and sleeping, or aisle seat for convenience and legroom!

**Tonu:** And then the agent hands you your boarding pass and says, 'Your flight boards at Gate 24B at four-thirty. Boarding will conclude fifteen minutes prior to departure. Have a wonderful flight!'

**Solu:** And you smile and say, 'Thank you very much, have a great day!' Practicing these short exchanges in your head before your trip eliminates eighty percent of airport anxiety.

**Tonu:** Now let's talk about surviving long-haul flights and beating jet lag! Jet lag can completely ruin the first three days of your vacation if you don't manage it.

**Solu:** Jet lag is that terrible exhaustion when your body's internal circadian clock is still set to your home time zone while the destination clock says it's three in the afternoon!

**Tonu:** So what is the secret to beating jet lag, Solu?

**Solu:** The moment you step onto the plane, set your watch and phone to the local time of your destination! If it is nighttime at your destination, force yourself to close your eyes and sleep on the plane.

**Tonu:** And when you land, never take a three-hour nap in the middle of the afternoon, no matter how desperately tired you feel!

**Solu:** Yes! Push through until at least nine or ten at night. Go outside into the natural sunlight, walk around, drink plenty of water, and let the sunshine reset your melatonin production.

**Tonu:** Natural sunlight is the most powerful jet lag remedy known to science. By day two, your body naturally aligns with the new time zone!

**Solu:** Another very important travel topic is staying safe and avoiding common tourist scams.

**Tonu:** Yes! Travelers are often prime targets for petty thieves and scammers because visitors are distracted looking at maps and scenery.

**Solu:** A classic scam in busy European squares is the friendship bracelet scam, or someone 'accidentally' spilling sauce on your coat so an accomplice can pick your pocket while pretending to help clean it!

**Tonu:** Or unmetered taxi drivers outside train stations who quote you five times the normal fare.

**Solu:** To protect yourself, always research common scams before arriving in a new city. Keep digital copies of your passport and visa stored securely in your email or cloud storage.

**Tonu:** And wear a cross-body bag or money belt under your shirt when walking through crowded markets or riding busy subways.

**Solu:** Street smarts and situational awareness keep you safe without making you paranoid. Most people in the world are genuinely kind and helpful!

**Tonu:** And let's talk about being a responsible, sustainable traveler.

**Solu:** Yes! The famous motto: 'Take only memories, leave only footprints.'

**Tonu:** Support local economies directly! Eat at family-owned cafes, buy handmade souvenirs from local artisans rather than imported plastic trinkets, and respect sacred cultural or religious sites by dressing appropriately.

**Solu:** When you treat local cultures with sincere respect and curiosity, the world opens its arms to you in ways you could never imagine.

**Tonu:** Now, let's explore our special English vocabulary section! Let's examine seven colorful, practical travel expressions and phrasal verbs that native speakers use all the time.

**Solu:** Let's do it! Tonu, what is our first expression for today?

**Tonu:** Our first expression is 'catch the travel bug'. To catch the travel bug means to develop a very strong passion or desire to travel and explore the world.

**Solu:** For example: 'Ever since her semester abroad in Spain, Emily has caught the travel bug and spends all her free time planning trips!'

**Tonu:** Once you catch that bug, there is no cure except buying another plane ticket! What is our second expression, Solu?

**Solu:** Our second expression is 'travel on a shoestring'. To travel on a shoestring means to travel with very little money, sticking to a tight and frugal budget.

**Tonu:** For example: 'During their gap year, the two students traveled on a shoestring across Southeast Asia, staying in budget hostels and eating street food.'

**Solu:** That is such a popular phrase among backpackers. And our third expression is 'off the beaten track', or 'off the beaten path'. This means visiting places that are quiet, uncommon, and far away from mainstream tourist crowds.

**Tonu:** For example: 'Instead of visiting crowded resort beaches, we decided to explore an off the beaten track mountain village.'

**Solu:** You find the most authentic culture when you venture off the beaten path! What is our fourth expression, Tonu?

**Tonu:** Our fourth expression is 'hit the road'. To hit the road is an informal way to say depart, start a journey, or begin a road trip.

**Solu:** For example: 'We need to hit the road by six in the morning if we want to beat the heavy holiday traffic!'

**Tonu:** And our fifth expression is 'culture shock'. Culture shock refers to the feeling of surprise, disorientation, or confusion someone experiences when encountering a very different culture or way of life.

**Solu:** For example: 'When Michael first moved from a quiet countryside town to Tokyo, he experienced massive culture shock because of the neon lights, crowds, and fast pace.'

**Tonu:** Culture shock is completely normal, and working through it helps you grow so much as an open-minded person.

**Solu:** And what is our sixth expression, Tonu?

**Tonu:** Our sixth expression is 'travel light'. To travel light means to pack only the bare essentials and avoid carrying heavy or bulky bags.

**Solu:** For example: 'I always prefer to travel light with just a small backpack so I can move easily through train stations and airports.'

**Tonu:** And our seventh expression is 'live out of a suitcase'. To live out of a suitcase means staying in hotels or temporary accommodations for a long period without settling down into a permanent home.

**Solu:** For example: 'As an international consultant, Robert has been living out of a suitcase for the past six months, flying to a new city every week.'

**Tonu:** Living out of a suitcase sounds glamorous at first, but it can get exhausting after a while!

**Solu:** These seven expressions are fantastic for anyone discussing holidays, vacations, adventures, or work trips.

**Tonu:** Now, Solu, let's talk about solo travel. A lot of people are terrified of traveling alone. Have you ever done a solo trip?

**Solu:** Yes, and I believe everyone should travel solo at least once in their lifetime! When you travel with friends or family, you naturally rely on each other and stay inside your bubble.

**Tonu:** That is so true. When you travel alone, you are forced to make decisions, talk to strangers, navigate foreign transit maps, and rely entirely on your own wits.

**Solu:** And you discover so much about your own inner strength! You realize, 'Hey, I can navigate an unfamiliar city by myself, order food in a language I barely speak, and handle unexpected challenges.'

**Tonu:** It builds tremendous self-confidence that transfers right back into your normal everyday life at home and at work.

**Solu:** Plus, you have complete freedom! If you want to wake up at noon and spend four hours sitting in a sidewalk cafe sipping tea and reading a novel, you don't have to ask anyone for permission!

**Tonu:** No negotiating itineraries, no waiting for someone else to get dressed! But on the other hand, traveling with loved ones has its own magic too.

**Solu:** Oh, of course! Sharing a spectacular sunset over the ocean with your partner or laughing together over a funny travel mistake creates memories that bond you forever.

**Tonu:** Now, let's talk about the role of the English language in travel. English is truly the global lingua franca of travel, isn't it?

**Solu:** Without question! No matter where you go in the world—whether it's an airport in Germany, a train station in Japan, a hotel in Brazil, or a market in Egypt—English is the common bridge that connects people.

**Tonu:** That is why practicing your English listening and speaking with us right now is the ultimate travel preparation! When you can communicate clearly in English, you never feel helpless when traveling abroad.

**Solu:** You can ask for directions, understand announcements at the gate, chat with fellow travelers, and handle any emergency with calm confidence.
