# Dreams, Goals & Personal Development
## Daily Speak English | English Listening & Speaking Practice

**Solu:** Hello hello, lovely listeners! Welcome back to Daily Speak English, where we learn to speak English naturally, fluently, and with unstoppable confidence. I'm Solu!

**Tonu:** And I'm Tonu! We are thrilled you are joining us today. Solu, let me ask you something: When you were a child, what was your biggest, wildest dream?

**Solu:** Oh, when I was seven years old, I was completely convinced that I was going to be an astronaut exploring Mars, or a world-famous wildlife photographer living in the African savannah!

**Tonu:** Children have such limitless imagination! They never say, 'Oh, becoming an astronaut sounds too difficult, the job market is competitive, and I might fail.'

**Solu:** Exactly! Children dream without fear. But somewhere along the journey to adulthood, people start trading their big dreams for safe, predictable routines.

**Tonu:** Yes! Society whispers into our ears: 'Be realistic. Settle down. Don't take risks.' And slowly, people bury their passions.

**Solu:** That brings us right into today's deeply motivational episode: Dreams, Goals, Personal Development, and becoming the person you were truly meant to be!

**Tonu:** I love this topic so much! Because personal growth is not a destination; it is an ongoing, lifelong journey.

**Solu:** So let's start with a foundational concept popularized by Stanford psychologist Dr. Carol Dweck: The difference between a Fixed Mindset and a Growth Mindset.

**Tonu:** Oh, this is such a transformative distinction! Solu, can you explain what a Fixed Mindset is?

**Solu:** A person with a Fixed Mindset believes that intelligence, talent, and abilities are fixed traits you are born with. You either have the gift or you don't.

**Tonu:** So if someone with a fixed mindset struggles to speak English, they think: 'I am just not good at languages. My brain wasn't built for this, so there is no point in trying.'

**Solu:** Precisely! They view failure as an identity: 'I failed, therefore I am a failure.'

**Tonu:** While someone with a Growth Mindset understands that the human brain is like a muscle that grows stronger with effort, challenge, and practice!

**Solu:** Yes! Neuroplasticity proves that your brain physically creates new neural pathways every time you learn a new skill. When someone with a growth mindset struggles with English, they say: 'I haven't mastered this yet, but with practice, I will get better every day!'

**Tonu:** The power of that tiny word: 'YET'! Adding 'yet' to any limiting belief changes everything. 'I cannot speak fluently... YET!'

**Solu:** Now let's talk about the difference between a dream and a goal. Why do so many dreams remain unfulfilled?

**Tonu:** Because a dream without a plan is just a wish! You can wish to write a book or learn fluent English for twenty years, but if you don't take daily action, twenty years will pass in the blink of an eye.

**Solu:** That is where structured goal setting comes in! A very famous and proven framework is SMART goals.

**Tonu:** Let's break down the SMART acronym for our listeners!

**Solu:** S stands for Specific: your goal must be clear and well-defined, not vague. Instead of 'I want to be fit', say 'I will run three miles three times a week.'

**Tonu:** M stands for Measurable: you need concrete numbers so you can track your progress. For example: 'I will learn twenty new English vocabulary words every week.'

**Solu:** A stands for Achievable: the goal should stretch you, but still be realistic given your current circumstances.

**Tonu:** R stands for Relevant: the goal must genuinely align with your core values and long-term life vision, not just something other people want for you.

**Solu:** And T stands for Time-bound: every goal must have a clear deadline! A deadline creates healthy urgency and prevents procrastination.

**Tonu:** Now, let's talk about James Clear's famous concept from Atomic Habits: The power of being one percent better every single day.

**Solu:** Oh, the mathematics of personal growth! If you improve by just one percent every day for 365 days, how much better do you become by the end of the year?

**Tonu:** People think you become three hundred and sixty-five percent better, but because of compounding, you actually become thirty-seven times better!

**Solu:** Thirty-seven times better! While if you get one percent worse every day, you decline almost down to zero.

**Tonu:** You don't need radical overnight transformations. You just need small, steady, consistent daily progress.

**Solu:** Now, let's address the elephant in the room: Procrastination! Why do we procrastinate on our biggest dreams?

**Tonu:** Most people think procrastination is due to laziness or poor time management, but modern psychology shows that procrastination is actually an emotional regulation issue!

**Solu:** Explain that, Tonu! That is fascinating.

**Tonu:** When a goal feels huge, difficult, or brings up fear of failure, our brain perceives it as a threat. So to protect us from discomfort, our brain urges us to do something easy and pleasurable instead—like scrolling social media or cleaning our room!

**Solu:** So procrastination is our brain avoiding unpleasant emotions! How can we beat procrastination, then?

**Tonu:** Use Mel Robbins' famous 'Five Second Rule'! When you have an instinct to work on a goal, count backwards: 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, and physically move before your brain has time to talk you out of it!

**Solu:** Or use the 'Two-Minute Rule': make the starting action so tiny that it takes less than two minutes!

**Tonu:** Yes! If you want to study English, don't tell yourself you have to study for two hours. Tell yourself you only have to open your notebook and read one single sentence.

**Solu:** Once you start, the friction disappears and momentum takes over naturally!

**Tonu:** Now let's talk about fear of failure and handling setbacks. What do you do when a major plan falls apart, Solu?

**Solu:** First, allow yourself to feel disappointed for a day; you are human! But then, shift into curiosity. Ask yourself: 'What is this setback trying to teach me?'

**Tonu:** Thomas Edison failed thousands of times before inventing the commercial light bulb. When a reporter asked him how it felt to fail thousands of times, what did Edison say?

**Solu:** Edison famously replied: 'I have not failed thousands of times; I have successfully found thousands of ways that will not work!'

**Tonu:** Every failure is simply valuable feedback. It eliminates the wrong paths and guides you closer to the right one.

**Solu:** Now, let's talk about building an action system rather than relying only on vision boards. A vision board with photos of mansions and tropical beaches is fun to look at, but vision boards don't do the push-ups!

**Tonu:** Exactly! Systems are the daily repeatable actions that produce the result. Focus on the inputs, and the outputs will take care of themselves.

**Solu:** If you are an aspiring author, your system is writing five hundred words every morning before breakfast. If you do that every day, you will finish a novel every year effortlessly.

**Tonu:** And celebrate your small wins along the way! Don't wait until you reach the final mountaintop to feel happy. Reward yourself for staying disciplined today.

**Solu:** How do you celebrate small wins, Tonu?

**Tonu:** When I finish a major chapter or complete a thirty-day challenge, I treat myself to a delicious dessert, buy that book I've been eyeing, or spend a guilt-free afternoon watching my favorite movie!

**Solu:** Rewarding your brain releases dopamine, which reinforces the desire to keep pursuing future goals!

**Tonu:** It creates positive emotional momentum that pulls you forward effortlessly.

**Tonu:** Let's also talk about the incredible power of having an Accountability Partner!

**Solu:** Oh, an accountability partner! The American Society of Training and Development found that having a specific accountability appointment with a partner increases your chances of achieving a goal to ninety-five percent!

**Tonu:** Ninety-five percent! That is astonishing. When you only keep a goal inside your own head, it is so easy to negotiate with yourself and quit on a cold morning.

**Solu:** Right! 'Oh, I am too tired today, I will start next Monday.' But when you know your accountability partner is waiting for your check-in text at eight in the morning, you get out of bed!

**Tonu:** And how should people handle critics, skeptics, and naysayers who mock their dreams?

**Solu:** Remember Theodore Roosevelt's famous speech, 'The Man in the Arena'! The credit belongs to the person who is actually in the arena, whose face is marred by dust and sweat, who strives valiantly.

**Tonu:** Not to the critic sitting comfortably in the cheap seats pointing out where the doer stumbled!

**Solu:** People who criticize your big dreams are almost always people who gave up on their own dreams. Never take advice about swimming from someone who never got into the water!

**Tonu:** And let's touch upon the 'Upper Limit Problem' described by author Gay Hendricks.

**Solu:** The Upper Limit Problem! That is such a psychological eye-opener. Why do people self-sabotage when things start going really well in their life?

**Tonu:** Because each of us has an internal thermostat for how much happiness, love, and success we believe we deserve!

**Solu:** So when life gets better than our old thermostat setting, our subconscious gets anxious and triggers an argument, gets sick, or makes a careless mistake to bring us back down to familiar comfort!

**Tonu:** Recognizing that self-sabotage allows you to consciously expand your capacity for joy, abundance, and success.

**Solu:** Now, let's explore a brilliant exercise: Designing your Ideal Ordinary Day!

**Tonu:** Oh, I love this visualization exercise! How does it work?

**Solu:** Instead of imagining winning a hundred million dollars in the lottery and lounging on a yacht forever, imagine an ideal ordinary Tuesday five years from now!

**Tonu:** What time do you wake up? What does the morning light look like in your room? What kind of work are you doing? Who are you having lunch with?

**Solu:** When you get crystal clear on what an ideal ordinary day looks and feels like, you can start reverse engineering your life right now!

**Tonu:** You realize that eighty percent of your ideal ordinary day doesn't require millions of dollars; it requires intentional choices, healthy boundaries, and daily discipline today.

**Solu:** And have you heard of the 'Twenty-Mile March' concept from business author Jim Collins?

**Tonu:** The Twenty-Mile March! In the historic race to the South Pole between explorers Amundsen and Scott, Amundsen's team committed to marching exactly twenty miles every single day, whether the weather was clear and sunny or a blinding blizzard!

**Solu:** While Scott's team rested during storms and marched forty miles on sunny days until they collapsed from exhaustion.

**Tonu:** And Amundsen's steady, disciplined twenty-mile march won the race safely!

**Solu:** Whatever your English or career goal is, commit to your daily twenty-mile march. Practice for fifteen or twenty minutes every single day, rain or shine!

**Tonu:** Now, let's talk about the Japanese philosophy of Ikigai: finding your purpose and reason for being!

**Solu:** Oh, Ikigai! It translates beautifully to 'the reason you wake up in the morning.'

**Tonu:** Ikigai is found at the intersection of four circles: What you love, what you are good at, what the world needs, and what you can be paid for!

**Solu:** When those four circles overlap, work ceases to feel like a tedious grind and transforms into meaningful, fulfilling service.

**Tonu:** And you don't have to find your complete Ikigai overnight! It reveals itself gradually as you try new projects, explore hobbies, and follow your curiosity.

**Solu:** And consider creating your own 'Personal Board of Advisors'!

**Tonu:** Just like a successful corporation has a board of directors, you can have three or four trusted mentors—one for career guidance, one for financial wisdom, and one for emotional well-being.

**Solu:** Whenever you face a major life crossroads, consult your personal board. Their combined perspective prevents blind spots and impulsive decisions.

**Solu:** Let's also talk about the powerful difference between intrinsic motivation and extrinsic motivation!

**Tonu:** Oh, that is such an important distinction for lifelong success and fulfillment!

**Solu:** Extrinsic motivation is doing something for external rewards or to avoid punishment—like working only for a paycheck, studying only to pass a test, or buying things to impress other people.

**Tonu:** While intrinsic motivation is doing something because you genuinely love it, find it fascinating, or it aligns with your deep personal values!

**Solu:** When you learn English only because someone forced you to, every study session feels like torture. But when you learn English because you love discovering global cultures, reading fascinating books, and making friends worldwide, practice becomes pure joy!

**Tonu:** Intrinsic motivation never runs out of fuel! It is like having an internal solar battery that powers you indefinitely.

**Solu:** And what about the psychological trap of the 'Comparison Trap'? In the era of social media, comparing yourself to others is the fastest route to misery.

**Tonu:** Teddy Roosevelt famously said: 'Comparison is the thief of joy!'

**Solu:** You are comparing your behind-the-scenes struggles and messy reality with someone else's curated, filtered, perfected highlight reel!

**Tonu:** The only person you should compare yourself to is the person you were yesterday. If you are one percent kinder, wiser, and more consistent than you were yesterday, you are winning!

**Solu:** And let's talk about the incredible benefit of having a 'Stop-Doing List'!

**Tonu:** A Stop-Doing List! Everyone has a To-Do list, but nobody has a Stop-Doing list! How does that work, Solu?

**Solu:** Personal development is as much about subtraction as it is about addition! Write down three habits you are actively stopping this month.

**Tonu:** For example: 'I will stop looking at my phone in bed. I will stop saying yes to social events I dread attending. I will stop complaining about traffic!'

**Solu:** When you remove energy-draining habits from your schedule, you suddenly unlock hours of free time and mental clarity for your biggest dreams!

**Tonu:** Now let's touch upon the power of morning priming and visualization!

**Solu:** Top Olympic athletes and successful entrepreneurs spend ten minutes every single morning visualizing their success in vivid sensory detail.

**Tonu:** When you visualize yourself delivering a confident English presentation, having a successful job interview, or completing a marathon, your brain fires the exact same motor neurons as if you were physically doing it!

**Solu:** It programs your subconscious nervous system to feel calm, prepared, and confident when the actual moment arrives.

**Tonu:** And finally, never underestimate the power of patience. Great trees take decades to grow deep roots before they reach the clouds!

**Solu:** Honor your journey, trust the process, and keep taking faithful daily steps toward your dreams.

**Solu:** Now, let's dive into our special English vocabulary section! Let's explore seven powerful everyday idioms and expressions related to dreams, ambition, and personal development.

**Tonu:** Let's do it! Tonu, what is our first inspiring expression today?

**Tonu:** Our first expression is 'reach for the stars'. To reach for the stars means to set ambitious, bold, and high goals for yourself, refusing to settle for mediocrity.

**Solu:** For example: 'Her parents always encouraged her to reach for the stars and pursue her passion for astrophysics.'

**Tonu:** Even if you miss the stars, you might land on the moon! What is our second expression, Solu?

**Solu:** Our second expression is 'step out of your comfort zone'. To step out of your comfort zone means to do things that feel unfamiliar, challenging, or slightly uncomfortable in order to grow.

**Tonu:** For example: 'Giving an English presentation in front of the entire company was terrifying, but stepping out of my comfort zone boosted my confidence immensely.'

**Solu:** Growth and comfort never coexist! What is our third expression, Tonu?

**Tonu:** Our third expression is 'bite off more than you can chew'. To bite off more than you can chew means to take on a task or responsibility that is too large, overwhelming, or difficult for you to handle.

**Solu:** For example: 'By agreeing to manage three major client projects simultaneously, David bit off more than he could chew and ended up feeling burned out.'

**Tonu:** Ambition is great, but pace yourself realistically! What is our fourth expression, Solu?

**Solu:** Our fourth expression is 'go the extra mile'. To go the extra mile means to make more effort than is expected or required of you in order to achieve excellence.

**Tonu:** For example: 'She always goes the extra mile for her students, staying after class to help anyone who needs extra explanation.'

**Solu:** People who consistently go the extra mile stand out in any career or community! And what is our fifth expression, Tonu?

**Tonu:** Our fifth expression is 'Rome wasn't built in a day'. Rome wasn't built in a day means that important achievements, complex skills, and great success take a long time and require patience.

**Solu:** For example: 'Don't get discouraged if you make mistakes while learning English; remember that Rome wasn't built in a day!'

**Tonu:** Patience and persistence are unbeatable virtues! What is our sixth expression, Solu?

**Solu:** Our sixth expression is 'stay the course'. To stay the course means to continue with a plan, goal, or commitment, even when facing severe difficulties or obstacles.

**Tonu:** For example: 'Despite economic downturns and market competition, the founders stayed the course and built a thriving business.'

**Solu:** When the road gets tough, stay the course! And what is our seventh expression, Tonu?

**Tonu:** Our seventh expression is 'turn over a new leaf'. To turn over a new leaf means to start behaving in a better, more positive, or more responsible way; to make a fresh start in life.

**Solu:** For example: 'After realizing his sedentary lifestyle was hurting his health, Marcus turned over a new leaf and began exercising and eating nutritious foods daily.'

**Tonu:** Every new morning is an opportunity to turn over a new leaf! These seven expressions are used constantly in English motivational writing, coaching, and everyday conversations.

**Solu:** Surround yourself with people who inspire you, dream big, encourage your goals, and push you to be your best self.

**Tonu:** And that includes the digital content you consume! Clean up your social media feed. Unfollow accounts that trigger jealousy or drama, and follow accounts that educate, inspire, and uplift you.
