# Public Speaking, Storytelling & Communication Confidence
## Daily Speak English | English Listening & Speaking Practice

**Solu:** Hello hello, wonderful listeners! Welcome back to Daily Speak English, where we help you speak English naturally, confidently, and with true charisma. I'm Solu!

**Tonu:** And I'm Tonu! We are so thrilled to be with you today. Solu, imagine you are standing backstage behind a heavy velvet curtain. In front of you is an auditorium filled with five hundred people waiting to hear you speak. How does your body feel?

**Solu:** Ooh, my palms would be slightly sweaty, my heart would be thumping in my chest, and my mouth might feel a little dry!

**Tonu:** You have just described Glossophobia—the fear of public speaking! Studies show that public speaking is the number one fear for most human beings, often ranking higher than the fear of spiders, heights, or even death!

**Solu:** Jerry Seinfeld famously joked: 'That means at a funeral, most people would rather be in the casket than giving the eulogy!'

**Tonu:** That is hilarious, but painfully relatable! And that brings us directly into today's empowering episode: Public Speaking, Storytelling Mastery, Vocal Presence, and Unshakable Communication Confidence!

**Solu:** Whether you have to give a presentation at your job, speak at a university seminar, deliver a wedding toast, or speak up in an English group discussion, today's episode will transform how you communicate.

**Tonu:** So let's start with reframing stage fright. Why do we feel that surge of adrenaline when speaking in front of a crowd?

**Solu:** Evolutionary biology! Thousands of years ago, if fifty pairs of eyes were staring at you across the plains, it meant you were about to be attacked by a rival tribe or a predator!

**Tonu:** So your sympathetic nervous system triggers the fight-or-flight response, pumping adrenaline through your bloodstream.

**Solu:** The secret is not trying to eliminate the butterflies in your stomach; the secret is teaching those butterflies to fly in formation!

**Tonu:** Physiologically, nervous excitement and fear feel identical: increased heart rate, heightened alertness. Reframe that sensation! Instead of telling yourself 'I am terrified', tell yourself: 'I am excited! My body is preparing me to deliver a powerful message!'

**Solu:** Now, let's talk about the single greatest secret of unforgettable speakers: The Art of Storytelling!

**Tonu:** Facts tell, but stories sell! If you present an audience with thirty bullet points of dry statistics and numbers, they will fall asleep in three minutes.

**Solu:** Our brains are hardwired for narrative. For tens of thousands of years before written language existed, human knowledge was passed down through stories told around the campfire!

**Tonu:** What are the four essential elements of a captivating story, Solu?

**Solu:** Element one is a relatable Character: someone the audience can see themselves in.

**Tonu:** Element two is a Conflict or Challenge: a problem, obstacle, or vulnerability that the character faces.

**Solu:** Element three is the Climax or Turning Point: the moment of decision, insight, or action where everything changes.

**Tonu:** And element four is the Resolution or Takeaway: the universal lesson that the audience can apply to their own lives!

**Solu:** Now let's talk about vocal variety and the magical power of the PAUSE!

**Tonu:** Many inexperienced speakers rush! They talk at lightning speed without breathing, filled with filler words: 'um, uh, like, you know, so.'

**Solu:** Why do people use filler words like 'um' and 'uh'?

**Tonu:** Because silence feels uncomfortable! When your brain is searching for the next word, silence feels like an eternity in your head.

**Solu:** But to the audience, a pause doesn't feel awkward at all! A pause conveys authority, confidence, and deliberate thought.

**Tonu:** When you finish an important sentence, pause for two seconds. Let the weight of the idea land in the listeners' minds.

**Solu:** And vary your vocal dynamics! Change your pace—speak quickly during exciting moments, and slow down to a whisper during an intimate, important insight.

**Tonu:** Now, let's talk about body language and nonverbal communication. Over sixty percent of human communication is nonverbal!

**Solu:** Avoid closed body language: crossing your arms tightly across your chest, slouching, or putting both hands in your pockets.

**Tonu:** Adopt an open posture! Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, pull your shoulders gently back, and use natural, open-palmed gestures that reinforce your spoken words.

**Solu:** And make genuine eye contact! Don't stare at the back wall or read word-for-word from your PowerPoint slides! Connect with individual faces in the room for three to five seconds at a time.

**Tonu:** Now let's roleplay delivering a heartfelt, memorable wedding toast or retirement speech!

**Solu:** A wedding toast! What is the three-part formula for a brilliant toast, Tonu?

**Tonu:** First: start with a warm, humorous personal story about the bride or groom that highlights their character.

**Solu:** Second: describe how their partner has brought out the very best in them since they met.

**Tonu:** And third: raise your glass and offer a sincere, touching wish for their future! For example: 'May your love be modern enough to survive the times, and old-fashioned enough to last forever. Cheers!'

**Solu:** Simple, elegant, heartfelt, and memorable!

**Solu:** Let's also explore vocal warm-up exercises that professional actors and keynote speakers use before stepping on stage!

**Tonu:** Oh, vocal warm-ups! Your voice is a physical instrument powered by vocal cords, the diaphragm, and facial articulators.

**Solu:** What is a great vocal warm-up exercise our listeners can practice right now, Tonu?

**Tonu:** Start with lip trills! Relax your lips and blow air through them making a motorboat sound: 'Brrrrrr!' It relaxes tension in your lips and jaw!

**Solu:** And practice vocal humming on an 'Mmm' sound, sliding your pitch gently up and down like a siren to wake up your vocal resonance chambers!

**Tonu:** And tongue twisters for crisp diction! Let's try a famous one: 'Unique New York, unique New York, you know you need unique New York!'

**Solu:** Or: 'Red leather, yellow leather, red leather, yellow leather!' Repeating tongue twisters trains your tongue, teeth, and lips to articulate consonants cleanly!

**Tonu:** Now let's talk about Impromptu Speaking—speaking off the cuff when you are suddenly put on the spot in a meeting or social gathering!

**Solu:** Many people freeze when a manager says: 'Solu, what is your opinion on this marketing campaign?'

**Tonu:** What is the secret framework to deliver a brilliant impromptu answer on the spot?

**Solu:** Use the PREP framework! P-R-E-P. Point, Reason, Example, Point!

**Tonu:** Let's walk our listeners through PREP!

**Solu:** P: State your main Point clearly in one sentence: 'I believe we should focus our marketing on short-form video content.'

**Tonu:** R: Give the Reason: 'Because our primary audience of young professionals consumes seventy percent of their media on mobile video.'

**Solu:** E: Share a concrete Example: 'For example, our competitor increased their lead conversion by forty percent after launching their video series last month.'

**Tonu:** P: Reiterate your main Point: 'Therefore, investing in video will deliver the highest return on our marketing budget!'

**Solu:** In sixty seconds, you have delivered a structured, persuasive, and completely coherent executive answer with zero preparation!

**Tonu:** And what should a speaker do when technical glitches happen—the microphone dies or the projector screen goes completely black?

**Solu:** Never panic or apologize repeatedly! Smile, project your voice naturally, and say with humor: 'It looks like the technology wanted to take a coffee break, but luckily my voice works without Wi-Fi!'

**Tonu:** Audiences love authentic, unflappable speakers who can laugh at unexpected hiccups.

**Tonu:** Now, let's explore our special English vocabulary section! Let's examine seven colorful idioms and expressions related to public speaking, performing, and communication.

**Solu:** Let's do it! Tonu, what is our first speaking expression today?

**Tonu:** Our first expression is 'break a leg'. 'Break a leg' is a traditional, affectionate idiom used in theater and performance to wish someone good luck before a speech or show.

**Solu:** For example: 'I know you're nervous about presenting to the executive board, but you're thoroughly prepared; break a leg!'

**Tonu:** What is our second expression, Solu?

**Solu:** Our second expression is 'speak your mind'. To speak your mind means to state your honest opinions, beliefs, and thoughts openly and directly, without fear.

**Tonu:** For example: 'In our team meetings, the manager always encourages everyone to speak their mind and offer creative ideas.'

**Solu:** What is our third expression, Tonu?

**Tonu:** Our third expression is 'stand out in a crowd'. To stand out in a crowd means to be extraordinarily distinctive, noticeable, and memorable compared to everyone else.

**Solu:** For example: 'Her charismatic storytelling and clear vocal delivery made her speech stand out in a crowd of fifty speakers.'

**Tonu:** What is our fourth expression, Solu?

**Solu:** Our fourth expression is 'read between the lines'. To read between the lines means to understand the hidden, deeper, or implied meaning that is not explicitly stated in words.

**Tonu:** For example: 'He said he was fine with the decision, but reading between the lines, I could tell he was disappointed.'

**Solu:** What is our fifth expression, Tonu?

**Tonu:** Our fifth expression is 'play it by ear'. To play it by ear means to improvise, deal with a situation as it develops spontaneously, rather than following a rigid predetermined plan.

**Solu:** For example: 'We don't know exactly how long the Q&A session will last, so let's just play it by ear.'

**Tonu:** And what is our sixth expression, Solu?

**Solu:** Our sixth expression is 'hit the nail on the head'. To hit the nail on the head means to describe a situation, problem, or truth with exact, perfect accuracy.

**Tonu:** For example: 'When you said the real problem is communication rather than software, you hit the nail on the head!'

**Solu:** And our seventh expression is 'steal the show'. To steal the show means to capture the most applause, attention, and admiration during a performance or event, even when you weren't the main star!

**Tonu:** For example: 'The youngest student gave such an energetic, heartfelt presentation that she completely stole the show!'

**Solu:** These seven expressions are used constantly in English media, corporate discussions, and arts reviews.

**Tonu:** Now, Solu, let's talk about handling difficult questions during a live Q&A session.

**Solu:** Many speakers dread the Q&A: 'What if someone asks a question I don't know the answer to?'

**Tonu:** Never bluff or pretend you know! If you make up a fake answer, experts in the audience will spot it instantly.

**Solu:** Instead, respond with poise and confidence: 'That is a fantastic and nuanced question! I don't have that specific data set in front of me right now, but I would love to look into it and follow up with you after the session.'

**Tonu:** That shows intellectual honesty, confidence, and genuine professionalism.

**Solu:** Or the monumental Pyramids of Giza in Egypt, standing tall against the desert sands for over four thousand years!

**Tonu:** And what about cultural words that have no direct translation in English? Untranslatable words reveal so much about cultural values!

**Solu:** Oh, untranslatable words! Like the Portuguese word 'Saudade'—a profound, bittersweet emotional longing for a person, place, or time that is lost and may never return.

**Tonu:** Or the southern African concept of 'Ubuntu'—'I am because we are!' The belief that our individual humanity is inextricably bound up in the humanity of others.

**Solu:** Or the German word 'Waldeinsamkeit'—the feeling of peaceful solitude and connection when walking alone in a quiet forest!

**Tonu:** Or the Spanish word 'Sobremesa'—the relaxed, beautiful time spent lingering around the table after a meal, talking with friends and family for hours!

**Solu:** Learning these foreign words expands our emotional vocabulary and deepens our appreciation for different worldviews.

**Tonu:** And what about the communal mate drinking ritual in Argentina, Uruguay, and Paraguay?

**Solu:** Yerba mate is shared from a hollowed calabash gourd through a metal straw called a bombilla. The gourd is passed around a circle of friends from hand to hand as a sacred gesture of trust and fellowship.

**Tonu:** You never wipe the straw, and saying 'Gracias' means you have had enough! It is a beautiful symbol of community.

**Solu:** Now let's explore the concept of the 'Circular Economy'!

**Tonu:** The Circular Economy! Our current global economic model is linear: Take, Make, Waste. We extract raw materials from the earth, manufacture products, and dump them in landfills!

**Solu:** While a circular economy mimics biological natural cycles: there is zero waste in nature! The fallen leaves of a tree become the rich nourishment for tomorrow's soil.

**Tonu:** In a circular economy, products are designed from day one to be easily disassembled, repaired, upgraded, and endlessly recycled back into manufacturing!

**Solu:** And what about regenerative agriculture and soil health?

**Tonu:** Healthy topsoil is literally the foundation of human civilization. Modern industrial farming with heavy chemical pesticides and intensive tilling has degraded one third of global arable land!

**Solu:** Regenerative farming practices—like cover cropping, rotational livestock grazing, and eliminating synthetic fertilizers—pull tons of carbon out of the atmosphere and store it back deep in the living soil!

**Tonu:** Living soil acts like a giant carbon sponge and retains rainwater, preventing droughts and catastrophic floods.

**Solu:** And did you know that over fifty percent of the oxygen we breathe is generated by marine phytoplankton in the oceans?

**Tonu:** Phytoplankton! Tiny microscopic marine algae produce more oxygen than all the tropical rainforests on Earth combined!

**Solu:** Protecting ocean health, stopping plastic runoff, and establishing marine protected areas is literally protecting the breath in our lungs.

**Tonu:** And let's talk about the liberating joy of Minimalism: owning less stuff and living more!

**Solu:** As the famous saying goes: 'We buy things we don't need, with money we don't have, to impress people we don't even like!'

**Tonu:** When you clear out physical clutter from your closets and home, your mental anxiety plummets. You realize that true wealth is experiences, relationships, and freedom, not piles of possessions.

**Solu:** And what about the simple thrill of growing your own food, even on a tiny apartment balcony?

**Tonu:** Planting a single pot of sweet basil, mint, or cherry tomatoes on a sunny windowsill connects you with the miracle of life. Plucking fresh basil to put on your homemade pizza is unbeatable!

**Solu:** Now let's talk about the critical first thirty seconds of a speech: The Hook!

**Tonu:** Oh, the hook! In our fast-paced modern world of shrinking attention spans, the first thirty seconds determine whether the audience listens with rapt attention or tunes out to look at their phones!

**Solu:** What are the three most powerful ways to hook an audience immediately, Tonu?

**Tonu:** Hook number one: Ask a provocative, thought-provoking question! For example: 'Raise your hand if you have ever had a dream that terrified you so much you almost gave up before starting?'

**Solu:** Hook number two: Share a startling, shocking statistic or counter-intuitive fact! For example: 'By the time I finish this five-minute speech, over three hundred thousand plastic bottles will be dumped into our oceans.'

**Tonu:** And hook number three: Start in media res—right in the middle of an intense, sensory story! 'It was two in the morning, the freezing mountain wind was howling, and our headlights suddenly went dead.'

**Solu:** Notice how none of those hooks start with: 'Good morning, my name is Solu, and today I am going to talk about...'

**Tonu:** Never start with boring administrative housekeeping! Hook their imagination first, and introduce yourself later!

**Solu:** And what about the 'Illusion of Transparency' in psychology?

**Tonu:** The Illusion of Transparency! When you are speaking on stage and your heart is beating fast, you feel like everyone in the room can see your internal anxiety!

**Solu:** You think: 'Oh no, they can see my knees shaking, they can hear my voice trembling!'

**Tonu:** In reality, over ninety percent of internal nervous sensations are completely invisible to the audience! To them, you look calm, composed, and articulate.

**Solu:** Knowing that truth gives you immense peace of mind. The audience wants you to succeed!

**Tonu:** And what about using humor effectively? You don't have to be a stand-up comedian!

**Solu:** Self-deprecating humor—laughing gently at your own minor quirks or past mistakes—is the most endearing form of humor. It humanizes you instantly.

**Tonu:** And finally, end with a powerful Call to Action! Tell the audience exactly what one step you want them to take today when they walk out the auditorium doors.

**Solu:** Now let's explore the famous rhetorical 'Rule of Three'!

**Tonu:** The Rule of Three! Human brains are biologically wired to recognize and remember triads: three words, three sentences, or three ideas!

**Solu:** Think about history's most famous phrases: 'Veni, vidi, vici—I came, I saw, I conquered!' Or 'Life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness!'

**Tonu:** Or 'Blood, sweat, and tears!' When organizing a speech or presentation, structure your arguments into three main pillars! It feels completely balanced and memorable.

**Solu:** And how should speakers eliminate unconscious nervous physical ticks—like pacing aimlessly, clicking a pen, or jingling keys in their pocket?

**Tonu:** Record a three-minute video of yourself rehearsing on your phone!

**Solu:** Watching yourself on video can be slightly uncomfortable, but it reveals unconscious physical habits in ten seconds.

**Tonu:** Once you see your nervous habits, you can consciously replace them with grounded, purposeful stance and intentional gestures.
